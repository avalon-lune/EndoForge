# EndoForge Phase 5 — authored anatomy assets

These are newly authored engineering assets for a local laparoscopic manipulation
scene. The editable source is `EndoForge_Anatomy.blend`; the reproducible authoring
script is `Scripts/BlenderPhase5/build_assets.py` in the project source tree.
The assets were authored and previewed in Blender 5.2.2 LTS.

## Reference use and origin

- The teacher-supplied Z-Anatomy atlas was reviewed for organ relationships,
  gallbladder shape, diaphragm, fascia and omental structures. Its inspected
  model preview and inspection report remain in `source/Z-Anatomy-inspection/`.
- The male anatomy atlas was opened without executing embedded scripts. Its
  gallbladder and liver parts were viewed in anterior and inferior inspection
  renders under `tmp/phase5-reference-review/`. The original file was not saved.
- CholecSeg8K VID35 and VID52 reference frames informed the pale gallbladder
  capsule, reddish-brown wet liver surface, fine vessels and yellow surrounding
  fatty tissue. No photographic pixels are used in these textures.
- The two research requirements informed independent geometry/material/camera
  versions, SI scale, stable material vertex identities, no baked highlights,
  and separation of geometric normals from shading detail.

All shipped vertices, UVs, textures and material nodes in this directory are
generated by the authoring script. No source-atlas mesh or source texture was
copied into the shipped assets. The anatomy is a simplified local working scene,
not a reconstruction of a patient or an asserted anatomical ground truth.
Teacher/clinician review of shape, orientation, attachment area and provisional
duct/arterial landmarks remains pending.

The hepatic support is one continuous asymmetric closed wedge, not a segmented
clinical liver model. Detailed lobar fissures, hilar anatomy and vascular trees
are outside this version. Adding those at a contact surface requires updating
the common physics mesh and repeating contact tests; an independently sculpted
visual gap must not be substituted over the tested solid support.

## Geometry and runtime interface

`physics-mesh.json` is the common geometry definition in metres: an outward
closed gallbladder middle surface, a 1.1 mm wall-thickness engineering setting,
neck constraints, liver-bed attachment identities and fixed anchor coordinates,
and the fixed hepatic support surface. Version 2 integrates a refined posterior
bed into the same closed liver volume. Each of the 31 anchors has an explicit
hepatic triangle ID and barycentric coordinates, checked against its actual
position. The bed is constructed 0.95 mm outward from the resting outer
gallbladder back wall, with a local transition into the surrounding support.
Material properties are not clinically
calibrated. Physics behavior and permitted manipulation ranges are verified by
the native service tests, not by this asset audit.

`scene.json` contains the same hepatic support vertices and faces, named local
cavity/omentum surfaces, provisional duct landmarks, texture paths, camera
framing, and the dynamic gallbladder surface's material-vertex binding. The
`attachment_bridge` field supplies boundary edges for a deforming connection
membrane between the OUTER gallbladder wall and fixed anchor coordinates on the
actual hepatic triangles. It uses the same wall layer as the native springs.

The cavity and omentum are deliberately open local surfaces. Their edges are not
accidental holes and they are not a complete abdominal or intestinal lumen.
The supplied camera-side opening makes the local operating field accessible.
Only the liver is marked as a fixed collision surface here; enclosing surfaces
outside the tested workspace are visual background until explicitly equipped
with physical contact and verified.

The Blender preview has an editable subdivision modifier on the gallbladder.
The UE runtime must use its recorded physical-surface mapping. This preview is
not a frame of the running simulation and is not a research ground-truth image.
Normal-map details are shading effects; they are not physical wall thickness,
geometry displacement or measured geometric curvature.

## Material controls

Each tissue has original 1024 x 1024 base-color, roughness and tangent-normal
textures. Base-color maps contain color/vascular variation but no precomputed
lighting or specular highlights. Shader roughness, coat and scattering settings
remain editable. The laparoscope preview uses two nearby area lights and a weak
inspection bounce light; this is a recorded artistic setup, not a calibrated
endoscope light source.

## Rebuild and check

Run Blender with `--background --factory-startup --disable-autoexec
--python-exit-code 1 --python Scripts/BlenderPhase5/build_assets.py` from the
project root. Existing physical geometry is loaded and preserved. The explicit
`-- --rebuild-physics` option regenerates it and invalidates physics receipts;
do not use that option without rerunning dependent physics tests.

First run Blender with `--background --factory-startup --disable-autoexec
--python-exit-code 1 --python Scripts/BlenderPhase5/verify_blend.py`. This reopens
the saved file natively and verifies editable objects, vertex groups, the display
modifier and all 21 linked textures. It writes `blend-verification.json`.

Then run `Scripts/BlenderPhase5/audit_assets.py` with Python to check geometry indices,
closed-surface edge incidence, scale/binding consistency, static liver identity,
texture CRC/full decompression and attachment-boundary mapping. The resulting
`asset-contract-check.json` identifies exact file hashes. These checks do not
establish tissue calibration, clinical effectiveness, continuous collision
freedom or an anatomically complete model.
